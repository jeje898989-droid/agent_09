## Eco-friendly trend in the public sector

- Seoul's Guro-gu runs eco-friendly banner boards at 20 main sites. They accept only banners made from fabric certified by the Ministry of Environment and printed with water-based ink. A banner can stay up for 30 days, against 15 days on a regular public board. All district departments and related public bodies may use them. (Seoul Shinmun, 2026-07-16, https://m.go.seoul.co.kr/news/2026/07/16/20260716020003)
- Public bodies are legally required to buy eco-friendly products. In 2022 they bought KRW 2.9499 trillion of eco-friendly products, or 7.2% of all goods purchased. This is the latest figure found. (e-Narajipyo, https://www.index.go.kr/unify/idx-info.do?idxCd=1376)
- General guides list canvas tote bags, stainless tumblers and cotton towels as sustainable promotional items. (FedEx, https://www.fedex.com/ko-kr/about/sustainability/get-inspired/eco-friendly-marketing-ideas.html)

## Legal caution: Improper Solicitation and Graft Act

- Souvenirs or promotional items handed out to an unspecified public are an exception under Article 8(3)(7).
- The exception does not apply when items go only to selected public officials, or when an item is too expensive by social standards to count as a souvenir. (Hankyung Business Q&A, https://magazine.hankyung.com/business/article/201609294789b)
- The KRW 50,000 limit applies to ordinary gifts, not to promotional items.
- Limits: the search found nothing on the Public Official Election Act. For a specific case, check an interpretation by the Anti-Corruption and Civil Rights Commission.
