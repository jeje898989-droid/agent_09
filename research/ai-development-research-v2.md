# AI Development: Research Notes (v2)

- Research date: 2026-10-04
- Scope: core technologies, industry and economic impact, regulation and ethics, outlook
- Period covered: 2024 – 2026

## 1. Core Technology Trends

### 1.1 Frontier model capabilities
- Industry produced over 90% of notable frontier models in 2025 (Stanford AI Index 2026).
- Several models now meet or exceed human baselines on PhD-level science questions, multimodal reasoning, and competition mathematics.
- Coding performance on SWE-bench Verified rose from about 60% to near 100% within a single year.
- **"Jagged frontier"**: the same models that win gold at the International Mathematical Olympiad read analog clocks correctly only 50.1% of the time. Headline benchmarks are a poor proxy for real-world behavior.

### 1.2 From chatbots to agents
- AI agents reached about 66% task success on the OSWorld computer-use benchmark, up from 12%.
- Gartner (2026 CIO survey): only 17% of organizations have deployed AI agents so far, but more than 60% expect to within two years.
- Use cases lead in coding, customer support (49% of teams) and operations (47%), followed by supply chain and financial planning.
- 84% of enterprise leaders expect to increase AI agent investment over the next 12 months.

### 1.3 Global competition
- The US–China model performance gap has effectively closed; the two have traded the lead several times since early 2025. As of March 2026, the top US model led by only 2.7%.
- The US still dominates investment and infrastructure: US private AI investment was $285.9B in 2025, more than 23 times China's $12.4B, and the US hosts 5,427 data centers, more than 10 times any other country.
- TSMC fabricates almost every leading AI chip, a concentration risk in the supply chain.

## 2. Industry and Economic Impact

### 2.1 Investment and adoption
- Global corporate AI investment reached $581.7B, up 130% year over year; generative AI investment rose almost fivefold to $170.9B.
- Generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet.
- 88% of surveyed organizations use AI.
- Estimated US consumer value of generative AI: $172B per year by early 2026.
- Over 80% of US high school and college students use AI for schoolwork.

### 2.2 Productivity
- PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer: firms in the most AI-exposed sectors recorded 34% productivity growth in 2025 versus 2018, compared with 24% for the least exposed.
- NBER / Atlanta Fed executive survey: large firms expect output per worker in 2026 to be about 3% higher than it would have been without AI.
- Workers with AI skills earn an average wage premium of 62% (up from 57%).

### 2.3 Labor market
- Aggregate employment effects are small so far: firms expect AI to reduce employment by less than 0.4% in 2026.
- Effects are concentrated on hiring pipelines and young workers in exposed jobs: employment of US software developers aged 22–25 fell nearly 20% from 2024.
- Opinion gap: 73% of experts expect a positive effect on jobs, versus 23% of the public.

### 2.4 Energy and infrastructure
- Data center electricity demand grew 17% in 2025, and AI-focused data centers grew 50%, far above the 3% growth of global electricity demand (IEA).
- IEA projects data center electricity use roughly doubling to around 945 TWh by 2030.
- Energy per AI task is falling by at least an order of magnitude per year thanks to hardware and software efficiency.

## 3. Regulation, Safety and Ethics

### 3.1 Incidents and trust
- Documented AI incidents rose from 233 (2024) to 362 (2025).
- Only about half of US middle and high schools have AI policies.
- Trust in government to regulate AI varies widely; the US reported the lowest level, at 31%.

### 3.2 EU AI Act
- Obligations for general-purpose AI (GPAI) model providers began on 2 August 2025.
- General application date: 2 August 2026, when most obligations, transparency duties and the AI Office's enforcement powers over GPAI providers took effect.
- GPAI providers must keep technical documentation, inform downstream providers, publish a training-data summary, and report serious incidents. The GPAI Code of Practice serves as a compliance route until harmonised standards exist.

### 3.3 Korea AI Basic Act
- Formal name: Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust. Passed on 26 December 2024; took effect on 22 January 2026.
- It combines industrial promotion with baseline duties: notifying users about AI-generated content, impact assessments and risk management for high-impact AI (healthcare, hiring, lending, energy), and a domestic representative for foreign providers.
- Enforcement is gentle: fines are capped at KRW 30 million, with a guidance period of at least one year.

## 4. Outlook and Key Issues
1. **Capability keeps accelerating**, but uneven ("jagged") performance means real-world validation matters more than benchmark scores.
2. **Agents are the next adoption wave**, yet 79% of organizations report adoption challenges (WRITER 2026 survey), so governance and change management are the bottleneck.
3. **Benefits are concentrated**: productivity gains favor AI-exposed "superstar" firms and skilled workers, while entry-level workers face the most pressure.
4. **Safety and policy lag behind capability**: rising incidents and regulation now moving into enforcement (EU, Korea).
5. **Energy and supply chains** (power, chips) are emerging as physical limits on AI growth.
